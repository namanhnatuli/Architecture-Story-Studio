# Research brief: How Gothic Cathedrals Reached Impossible Heights

- Opened: 2026-11-05. Audience: international English-speaking YouTube viewers; the film is in English.
- Format: 10–18 min (owner's decision of 2026-11-05, `project.json` → `runtime_decision`), 16:9, 24 fps, Studio visual style, illustrative models.

## Central question
How did medieval builders, working without modern structural calculations, raise stone vaults more than 40 meters high, and why did some of them come down?

## Research questions
1. What problem does a stone vault create (thrust), and how did Romanesque builders answer it?
2. What does each Gothic device do: pointed arch, rib vault, flying buttress? Where do they come from, and are they really Gothic inventions?
3. How did builders work without modern structural theory (geometry, templates, centering, carpenters)?
4. Where was the limit? What happened at Beauvais (1284, 1573) and at Amiens (the iron chain), and what is still debated?
5. Why do these buildings stand, according to modern engineers (stability rather than strength, line of thrust)?
6. Which common misunderstandings must the film avoid ("first", "revolution", exact heights, one cause for 1284)?
7. What can the existing asset library show? Nothing Gothic yet: a new cathedral family, 2D diagrams and rights-cleared photographs are needed.

## Source policy
- Prefer government agencies, universities, engineering institutions, preservation bodies, primary documents.
- Every claim: verbatim excerpt + locator in `research/sources.json` (SOURCE_VERIFIED). Never read a source you could not open.
- No invented quantities (loads, stresses, capacities, safety factors, rankings). No "linear progress" story, no "one type is best".
- Images: only with a complete rights record (public domain / compatible licence, creator, source URL, original record).
